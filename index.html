Research

I work at the interface between quantum information theory, quantum field theory and gravity. Much of my research uses information-theoretic quantities—entanglement entropy, relative entropy, mutual information and modular Hamiltonians—to learn about the structure of quantum field theories and about how spacetime geometry emerges in the AdS/CFT correspondence.

Selected publications

Euclidean volume fluctuations in de Sitter quantum gravity
D. Blanco, G. Pérez-Nadal, B. Sivilotti
JHEP 07 (2026) 244arXivDOI

We obtain the probability distribution of the total volume of the universe in Euclidean de Sitter quantum gravity from the partition function, at several levels of approximation.

It turns the dependence of the partition function on the cosmological constant into a concrete statistical statement about the size of the universe: quantum effects consistently favor smaller universes.

Modular Hamiltonian of a chiral fermion on the torus
D. Blanco, G. Pérez-Nadal
Phys. Rev. D 100, 025003 (2019)arXivDOI

We compute the modular Hamiltonian of a chiral fermion at finite temperature on a circle, using a simple method of images.

The result is non-local even for a single interval: the first known example of a modular Hamiltonian with this behavior.

Local temperatures and local terms in modular HamiltoniansEditors’ Suggestion
R. Arias, D. Blanco, H. Casini, M. Huerta
Phys. Rev. D 95, 065005 (2017)arXivDOI

We define analogues of the Unruh temperature for any quantum field theory and region using relative entropy. Among other things, we argue that in two dimensions they come from a universal local term in the modular Hamiltonian.

Selected as a Physical Review D Editors’ Suggestion and featured on the journal’s front page.

Localization of negative energy and the Bekenstein bound
D. D. Blanco, H. Casini
Phys. Rev. Lett. 111, 221601 (2013)arXivDOI

We show that in a conformal field theory negative energy cannot be isolated far from positive energy, and prove a new form of the Bekenstein bound based on monotonicity of relative entropy.

It connects quantum energy inequalities with quantum information through relative entropy.

Relative entropy and holography
D. D. Blanco, H. Casini, L.-Y. Hung, R. C. Myers
JHEP 08 (2013) 060arXivDOI

We study relative entropy in AdS/CFT. Among other things, we formulate the first law of entanglement, ΔS = ΔH, and check it and the related inequalities for spherical regions.

With more than 400 citations, it is my most cited work: the first law of entanglement was later used to derive the linearized Einstein equations from the entanglement of the boundary theory.
