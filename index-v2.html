Research

I work at the interface between quantum information theory, quantum field theory and gravity. Much of my research uses information-theoretic quantities—entanglement entropy, relative entropy, mutual information and modular Hamiltonians—to learn about the structure of quantum field theories and about how spacetime geometry emerges in the AdS/CFT correspondence.

Recent topics include entanglement and modular Hamiltonians of chiral fields on the torus, modular conjugation for multicomponent regions, energy inequalities derived from relative entropy, and volume fluctuations in Euclidean de Sitter quantum gravity.

JHEP 2013

Relative entropy and holography

Formulated what is now called the first law of entanglement, a natural extension of the first law of thermodynamics.

Phys. Rev. Lett. 2013

Localization of negative energy and the Bekenstein bound

Properties of relative entropy yield quantum energy inequalities, linking quantum information and high-energy physics.
